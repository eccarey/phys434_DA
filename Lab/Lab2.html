Mathematical

Poisson Distribution: $p_i(x) = \frac{\lambda^ke^{-\lambda}}{k!}$

Summing Poisson Distributions: $\sum_{j=1}^{n}p_i(x) = \frac{\lambda^ke^{-n\lambda}}{k!}$ where $n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$

Example: $p_1(x) + p_2(x)$ $ = \frac{\lambda^ke^{-\lambda}}{k!} + \frac{\lambda^ke^{-\lambda}}{k!}$ $ = \frac{\lambda^k}{k!}(e^{-\lambda} + e^{-\lambda})$ $ = \frac{\lambda^ke^{-2\lambda}}{k!}$

Conceptually

If you have a unit thing per unit time (a rate) and want to combine it with a seperate rate of the same units, you get the two rates added together. For instance, my cat eats 5 times per day (Binx...) and my other cat eats 2 times per day (Guen...). Together, they eat 7 times per day.

Width: In part (c), we are taking the k-values from part (b) and dividing them by the number of days. The width of the distribution should "shrink" to be the same as day 1. This is seen through each distribution being centered around k = 5.

Height: As seen in part (b), each successive day results in a distribution that is less tall. Since only the k-values are changing, then the heights of distributions should remain the same as in part (b).

        dist_n = np.convolve(dist_n, dist)
